# Privacy and networking

Ojo de Gato is a camera pointed at the inside of your home. The networking design starts from that fact.

## The stance

- **No cloud. No accounts. No third-party video service.** There is nothing to sign up for and no server of ours involved. The pipeline runs entirely on your Mac.
- **Footage never leaves your machines.** Recordings and motion frames live in `~/.ojogato/` and are deleted on a rolling window (48h / 9 GB by default). The live stream is only reachable from devices you own, over your tailnet.
- **Tailscale-first.** Remote viewing rides your existing [Tailscale](https://tailscale.com) network — an encrypted WireGuard mesh between your own devices. No accounts beyond the ones you already have, no ports opened to the internet.

The one deliberate egress is **Telegram**: snapshots, clips, and alerts you asked for are sent to your chat via the Telegram Bot API over HTTPS. If that trade-off doesn't suit you, don't configure the Telegram token — the live view and local recordings work without it.

## How the ports are laid out

| Service | Bind | Reachable from |
|---|---|---|
| RTSP (internal transport) | `127.0.0.1:8554` | This Mac only |
| HLS (live view) | `:8888` | Your tailnet |
| WebRTC (live view) | `:8889` | Your tailnet |

**RTSP is loopback-only, by design.** It is the raw internal bus — the recorder, motion detector, and snapshot tool all read from `rtsp://127.0.0.1:8554/cam`. It is never exposed off the machine, and there is no configuration option to expose it.

**HLS and WebRTC bind all interfaces**, which in practice means: your LAN and your Tailscale address. The live URL that setup configures (`OJOGATO_LIVE_URL`) uses your Tailscale MagicDNS name, e.g. `http://your-mac.your-tailnet.ts.net:8889/cam`. From your phone, turn Tailscale on and the URL works from anywhere; without Tailscale, it doesn't resolve. That's the access control model: membership in your tailnet.

## Never port-forward

Do not forward ports 8888/8889 (or any Ojo de Gato port) on your router, and do not put them behind a public reverse proxy. The streams carry live video of your home with no authentication layer of their own — the security model is *network reachability*, and it only holds if reachability stays private. Tailscale gives you remote access without ever opening a port; there is no scenario where port-forwarding is the right tool here.

If you want to harden further:

- Use Tailscale ACLs to restrict which of your devices can reach ports 8888/8889 on the camera Mac.
- Keep the Mac's macOS firewall on; the launchd services need no inbound rules beyond mediamtx.
- `ojogato off` pauses the entire stack (creates `~/.ojogato/PAUSED`) when you want the camera verifiably idle — the heartbeat respects it too.

## What data exists, and where

| Data | Location | Lifetime |
|---|---|---|
| Rolling recordings (10-min mp4 segments) | `~/.ojogato/recordings/` | 48h, capped at 9 GB (configurable) |
| Motion frames (timestamped JPEGs) | `~/.ojogato/motionframes/` | Rolling cleanup |
| Snapshots / clips built for reports | `~/.ojogato/frames/` | Hours |
| Config incl. Telegram token | `~/.ojogato/config.env` (chmod 600) | Until you delete it |
| Snapshots/clips you requested | Your Telegram chat | Telegram's retention, under your account |

Nothing is uploaded anywhere else, ever. `ojogato uninstall` removes the services and the `~/.ojogato/` directory.

## The MCP server (optional, off by default)

`OJOGATO_MCP_ENABLED="1"` starts `com.ojogato.mcp`, which lets an MCP-capable agent (Claude Code, Hermes, Codex) read what the pipeline knows: status, verified events, a live snapshot, an event's best frame, contact sheets of clips. Every tool is read-only — none can switch the camera off, change config, or run setup. Design: [`docs/mcp-server.md`](mcp-server.md).

| Exposure | Default | What controls it |
|---|---|---|
| Bind address | `127.0.0.1:8790` (this Mac only) | `OJOGATO_MCP_BIND`: `127.0.0.1`, `tailscale` (the Mac's tailnet IP, nothing else), or one explicit IP. `0.0.0.0` is refused, by the server and by `ojogato doctor`. |
| `/mcp` (tools) | bearer token required | `OJOGATO_MCP_TOKEN` — 64 hex chars from `openssl rand -hex 32`, generated by install / `ojogato setup`, stored in `config.env` (chmod 600). `ojogato mcp token` prints it. |
| `/media/…` (images, clips for your phone) | signed links only | Each link carries an expiry and an HMAC-SHA256 signature made with the token; it expires after `OJOGATO_MCP_MEDIA_TTL` (1 h) and can only name files under `~/.ojogato/frames/`. A phone can open it without a header; nobody can guess one or walk out of `frames/`. |

The same rule as the live view applies: **tailnet only, never port-forward 8790.** For a remote agent (say, Hermes on a VPS), put that server on your tailnet and use `OJOGATO_MCP_BIND="tailscale"`; `ojogato mcp url` prints the address to give it.

**What an agent may send to its own cloud model.** Facts (counts, times, verdicts) come from the on-device pipeline as JSON. Images — a snapshot, an event frame, a contact sheet — are handed to the agent you connected, and what it does next is up to that agent. If it runs on a cloud LLM, then *that agent* is sending a frame of your home to *its* model provider, under that provider's terms. Ojo de Gato itself still uploads nothing. Video never goes through MCP: the agent gets a signed tailnet link for you to tap, or ojogato sends it to your Telegram itself. If frames of your home shouldn't reach a cloud model, connect only a local agent, or leave the server off.
